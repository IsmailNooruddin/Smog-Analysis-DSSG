Milestone 01: Team Formation, Problem Framing & Data Curation

Milestone 01 focuses on forming your team, framing a social problem, and developing a dataset around that problem. An important part of this milestone is the data curation process.
Data Curation

In real-world social-impact and research settings, data is rarely available in a perfectly clean, labeled, and model-ready format. Questions of where data comes from, who collected it, who is represented or missing, and what additional work is needed before it can be meaningfully analyzed are therefore an important part of the data science process.

For this milestone, teams are expected to demonstrate meaningful engagement with the construction or curation of their dataset. This could take several forms:

    Scraping and structuring: Publicly available information from websites, YouTube, government portals, NGO reports, or social media APIs can be collected and organized into a usable dataset.

    Combining and reconciling: Two or more existing datasets may be brought together to create a new analytical resource—for example, linking district-level health information with census data or combining satellite metadata with ground-truth survey data.

    Structuring unstructured information: PDFs, scanned documents, audio, or video can provide valuable information that requires transcription, annotation, extraction, or other forms of structuring before analysis.

    Extending the use of an established dataset: Established datasets such as World Bank, HIES, or PSLM data can also be appropriate when the analysis introduces a meaningful additional dimension. This might involve harmonizing variables across years, examining an unusual combination of variables, or investigating potential fairness or representation issues.

The emphasis is therefore not necessarily on collecting completely new data, but on demonstrating that the team has made a meaningful contribution to how the data is assembled, structured, combined, interpreted, or extended. A dataset downloaded directly from Kaggle, followed by standard preprocessing and modeling, would generally not provide sufficient evidence of this curation component on its own, and the project proposal might even be returned for review.
Dataset Extensibility

The choice of problem and dataset at this stage will also influence a later component of the semester. Around Week 14, teams will work with additional data and examine how incorporating it affects an already-trained model.

This makes the extensibility of the dataset an important consideration from the beginning. Ideally, the selected problem should allow for additional observations or complementary information to become available later; for example, through a second round of data collection, a newer data release, another geographic area, or a related data source.

A useful question to consider when selecting a problem is:

What additional data could reasonably become available later that would allow the initial analysis or model to be revisited?

Problems that naturally allow for such an extension will provide greater flexibility for the later milestone.
Team Formation

Each team should consist of 03 members. Teams are encouraged to be interdisciplinary where possible, bringing together different academic backgrounds, skills, or perspectives. Where an interdisciplinary team is not possible, teams should still aim to divide responsibilities in a way that brings multiple perspectives to the project.
Milestone 01 Submission

Maximum: 2 pages

The submission should briefly cover:

    Team: Team members and their disciplinary backgrounds (CS / SDP / other)

    Social problem: The problem being addressed and its connection to relevant SDG(s)

    Dataset: Its source, how it was or will be obtained, the curation involved or planned, scope (records, variables, time frame), and limitations

    Initial hypothesis: The relationships or patterns the team expects to investigate

    Future extension: A brief description of how the dataset could potentially be extended or augmented later in the semester

The purpose of this milestone is to establish a strong foundation for the project. Dataset choices that rely entirely on an off-the-shelf Kaggle dataset without a clear additional curation or analytical dimension may need to be revised before the milestone can be assessed.

Teams are welcome to discuss their proposed problems and dataset ideas during office hours before the submission deadline. An early discussion can be particularly useful given how strongly these initial choices shape the subsequent stages of the project.

Good luck, and think ambitiously.
